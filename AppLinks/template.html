‘What are we doing?' After 400+ cancer cases reported, NJ community demands answers

Neighbors in a New Jersey community gathered for a crisis meeting to get more information about a possible cancer cluster after more than 400 cases had been reported — including dozens on just one street.

There was a moment of silence and a prayer Thursday evening for those in Keyport who had lost their life to cancer or continue to suffer.

It served as a time to reflect for the crowd who came together to demand the state do more toxic chemical testing on and around the old Aeromarine landfill site that many fear may have been seeping into the Raritan Bay and ground soil.

1.5 million hand warmers recalled after woman's death, 15 fires, 350 burn injuries

Nearly 2 million hand warmers sold online for years at stores like Amazon and Walmart have been recalled after a woman’s death, hundreds of reported burn injuries, and more than a dozen fires, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission announced Thursday.

The federal agency says the Chinese manufacturer yanked the hand warmers over concerns the lithium-ion batteries can overheat and ignite.

According to the CPSC, the manufacturer, Ocoopa Direct, has received 1,480 reports of hand warmers overheating, resulting in 15 fires, 350 burn injuries, and the February death of an 83-year-old San Diego woman.

7th death confirmed in Manhattan Legionnaires' outbreak, matching total from 2025 outbreak

New York City health officials confirmed a seventh death in connection to the Legionnaires’ outbreak on the Upper East Side, matching the number of lives lost in a similar outbreak in Harlem in 2025.

The seventh death was announced Thursday evening, one of the six people who was still hospitalized from the illness, according to the Health Department. The silver lining of good news: Officials have repeatedly said no new cases had been found since July 21.

As of Thursday, there were still five people in the hospital from Legionnaires’.

Police issue warning about abandoned NJ movie theater

Break-ins and vandalism at an abandoned movie theater in New Jersey have triggered warnings and a crackdown by police, who believe the problem is being driven by a growing trend on social media.

Films stopped showing at the former Xscape movie theater in Howell Township in 2021, but there’s still drama happening inside.

“A lot of my friends, I know personally, have been inside. They like the thrill of it,” said Josh Krutyansky, who lives in the town.

Mom and grandma charged with murder after Bronx teen with autism starved to death

A mother and grandmother face murder charges after a teen with autism starved to death, and his twin brother was found severely malnourished inside their Bronx apartment, according to police sources.

On March 19, EMS took 16-year-old Adonis Jarvis to NYC Health + Hospitals/Lincoln. When they found him inside the the Mott Haven apartment on East 147th Street, he weighed just 59 pounds, an investigation found.

The teen was pronounced dead at the hospital, police said. His death was ruled a homicide, with the medical examiner determining Adonis died as a result of neglect, starvation and dehydration, according to the district attorney’s office.

Snacky? New MTA vending machines may be coming to a subway station near you

The MTA is rolling out vending machines at more than two dozen subway stations in the next year and a half, offering the hundreds of thousands of people who pass through them each day new snack and drink options.

Three of them are already up and running — at the 2nd Avenue F train station, the 23rd Street R and W station, and the 116th Street 6 station. The MTA is setting up shop inside former retail spaces, so the machines fit within the existing stations’ framework and don’t crowd already-busy platforms and walkways.

CC Vending, Inc. manages them. At some point, the company can choose to add more offerings, including retail merchandise, tech, health and beauty products, and other handy on-the-go items.
